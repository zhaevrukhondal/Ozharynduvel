# Ozharynduvel

> An empty carriage follows the hoofprints of a storm that has not yet arrived.

## The Road Across the Marsh

The road crosses the marsh on stones that cannot be seen from its banks. A traveler must take the first step before the next stone appears. The water remains shallow until somebody attempts to measure it.

At the center of the crossing stands a waiting room with three doors. One opens toward the road already traveled. One opens toward the road still ahead. The third has no handle, and its keyhole contains a small piece of cloudy sky.

An empty carriage stops outside every evening. Its wheels are white wood, its windows dark green glass. No horses draw it. Nevertheless, fresh hoofprints mark the road behind it, filled with water warmer than the surrounding marsh.

The attendant polishes the carriage step and sets a cup of tea inside. By dawn, the tea is cold and a black feather rests across its rim.

## A Traveler With a Paper Ticket

A traveler arrived carrying a ticket that had grown from the pages of a book. He had fallen asleep while reading about a journey through snow. On waking, he found the ticket between his fingers and the book filled with descriptions of his childhood home.

The attendant held it against the waiting room's lamp. Through the paper appeared a small landscape: a hill, a bare tree, and a house with smoke rising into a sky without wind.

He asked whether the traveler remembered leaving that house. The traveler said he remembered a carriage window, a cold sleeve against his cheek, and someone promising to return before the first snow melted.

The attendant placed the ticket under the cup. The green windows cleared. Inside the carriage, a seat retained the shape of a person who had just stood up.

## The Passenger Behind the Glass

The traveler climbed aboard. Through one window he saw the marsh. Through the other, the snowy hill from the ticket. At the far end of the carriage sat a woman whose face was hidden behind a small book.

Her sleeves were dark blue. A patch of frost lay on one shoulder. As she turned a page, the traveler heard a door closing in the house he had once called home.

He sat opposite her. The carriage moved without disturbing the cup. Outside, hoofprints appeared in the air ahead, each holding a little rain. Their line climbed gradually above the marsh until the waiting room looked like a lantern floating in black water.

The woman lowered the book. Where her face should have been, he saw the reflection of his own face as a child, asleep against a cold blue sleeve.

## What Drew the Carriage

The hoofprints belonged to no horse. Far ahead, a long creature moved through the rain, its legs touching places the road had not yet reached. Its scales were the color of wet slate. Antlers curved around its head like the branches of an overturned tree.

Its wings remained folded until the carriage reached the first cloud. Then one opened, revealing a landscape of white fields beneath its membrane. Tiny houses stood among them, each with smoke rising straight into the dark.

The traveler recognized the hill again. He tried to point it out, but the woman's book opened by itself. A page turned toward him. On it was a picture of the waiting room, with its third door standing open.

Beyond that door lay a bed covered in blue cloth. Someone small was sleeping there, holding a paper ticket against their chest.

## The Writing Inside the Wheel

The carriage stopped in a place that resembled the silence after thunder. The attendant stood outside, though the traveler had not seen him leave the marsh. He knelt beside a wheel and brushed rain from its inner rim.

Letters showed beneath the water:

*Ozha — ryn — duvel — khaen.*

The woman spoke them. Her voice was familiar enough to hurt, but the traveler could not attach it to a name. The cloudy sky inside the waiting room's keyhole became visible in the carriage window, then widened until it filled the glass.

He understood that the third door had never been locked. It had been waiting for somebody on the other side to wake.

He placed his ticket inside the woman's book. She closed it gently, and the cup on the small table released its first thread of steam.

## A Journey Remembered Differently

When he stepped down, the waiting room stood at the edge of morning. The marsh reflected a pale sky. Behind the third door, somebody stirred beneath blue cloth and turned toward a window.

The attendant took the cup from the carriage. There was no feather across its rim. Instead, a small paper leaf floated on the tea, covered in handwriting too fine to read.

The traveler walked toward the far bank. Stones appeared beneath his feet before he needed them. At the end of the crossing stood the bare tree from the snowy hill, its branches carrying the first green buds.

He looked back once. The carriage windows were dark again, but a blue sleeve rested against one pane.

Above the marsh, the coming storm opened its wings and continued along a road nobody below could see.
